The White Paper.

What PolitíCat is building, how the site stays organized, and what's done versus still ahead. Updated as work ships — check the boxes, keep the receipts.

1. What PolitíCat is

PolitíCat NM is nonpartisan civic education for New Mexico, hosted by Don Gato Cívico. It shows what public records exist, what they say, and what is still unknown — then points you to the official source so you can verify it yourself. It is not a party, not a campaign, and not a government agency. Records first. Interpretation disciplined. Uncertainty visible.

2. How it's built

Two products with a hard boundary between them:

  • Civic Substrate (the engine, private): collects public records, preserves the original documents with cryptographic fingerprints, tracks what changed, and prepares approved publication objects.
  • PolitíCat (this site): publishes only approved, non-sensitive information — county pages, maps, explainers — always linking back to the source document. PolitíCat never touches raw evidence or personal data stores.

Every published claim carries one of four honest states — VERIFIED (the exact statement is supported by captured primary evidence), DEGRADED (evidence exists but something material is missing or stale), UNKNOWN (the record checked doesn't establish the answer), or REFUSED (we deliberately decline to render a verdict, and say why). No confidence percentages, ever. And one rule above all: verifying that somebody said something is not verification that it's true. A receipt means "this is the document we captured from this source on this date" — never "this claim is true."

3. How the site stays organized (the anti-sprawl canon)

The rule: new work joins an existing hub. A new top-level page requires updating this white paper first, with a reason. Geographic anything becomes a layer on the map, not a new page. One topic = one dossier page in The Trail, not a scatter of fragments. Edge-to-edge layout is canon: no pixel cap, ever. The layout fills 100% of every screen — phone, tablet, laptop, 65-inch — with gutters only; density scales by adding columns and panels, never by centering a boxed column in dead space.

Navigation canon (adopted Aug 15, updated Aug 16, 2026): the top nav is Explore NM · Elections · Pulse · Learn · The Trail · Community · About, plus the persistent "Ask a Question" button (the only Ask entry). This white paper lives under About and in the footer. Explore owns the county terminal, county pages, and the map; Elections owns election action and the practice ballot; Pulse owns the civic feed (preview until scheduled collection lands); Learn owns evergreen explainers.
